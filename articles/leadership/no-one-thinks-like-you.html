On Assumptions

No One Thinks Like You.

The most expensive assumption in business and in life is believing other people process the world the same way you do.

By Jared Scott

I generally refrain from absolute terms such as Always, Never, or Impossible. That being said, "No One Thinks Like You". How can I be so certain of this statement? After all, are there not thousands of likes of that picture you posted on your favorite social media site? Didn't you get told by so many individuals what a wonderful job you did on that last project? Or what about the multitudes of folks that love the same toppings as you do on pizza? Surely that is evidence of thinking the same, is it not? Having similar likes or identical opinions on various things does not in itself mean that we think the same.

Social media has been a major contributor to providing us a scale of who thinks like us and who does not. For example, do you like the picture at the header of this article? Yes or No? That's easy to answer, but, what if I asked what colors stand out the most to you in that picture? What color are the clouds? Now those questions will elicit a multitude of opinions, responses, counter responses, and on and on. It is likely that no two opinions will match completely.

There is a tendency to project how we feel about a matter onto others. Those feelings can vary based on how our day is going. For instance, have you ever received an email or text and you projected your current feelings about that person or how you were doing at that moment onto the sender? Of course you have. We all are guilty of that from time to time. Come to find out the person's message was not intended to be taken as we took it. Clarity is then needed to really understand how others think about things and we will not search for that clarity if we think others think like us. Remember, "No One Thinks Like You".

Let me give you an example of this. Last year I asked my department if they felt that you should return a missed call if the caller did not leave a voicemail. The responses were not what I expected and they varied from yes to no, or it depends. Most of the responses were not the same as how I would have handled this scenario. There were definitely some assumptions on my part. We are not going to digress into a sidebar conversation about what the right answer is, in fact, is there a right answer?

Here's the danger of thinking that others think like you do. We start to make assumptions and assumptions lead to communication gaps and communication gaps can lead to resentment or an unsettled mental state. The climate we live and work in today has created divides and rifts if we don't think the same or have the same likes/dislikes. We want to feel like we are ok and so we group ourselves into other groups that are similar in thinking and opinions. This then feels like a safe comfort zone. In reality this creates an echo chamber that reassures us and doesn't help us to grow.

“If everyone is thinking alike, then somebody isn't thinking".”George S. Patton

One of the areas where this can rear its ugly head are in Customer relationships. If we are providing goods and/or services as a company it is so vital to know what they are thinking. The easiest way to find this out is to ask. You will see this in the form of surveys, but does a survey really convey what the customer is thinking? Surveys are often times filled out on a whim without providing genuinely useful information. It takes effort to get to the real issues with a customer. This effort means meeting with the customer face to face, even if virtually. You have to find out: What is really important to them? What makes them want to do business with you? How much do they really know about what your company has to offer? You see, we know everything about our company, what is good, what is awesome, where our weak spots are, how we operate, and what we have to offer. The problem is that we assume that our customers know all of these fine details; after all, they are our customer, right?

One of the biggest mistakes we make is thinking that other people think the same way we think and know the same things that we know.

As I have discussed in another article I have wrote, communication is key. This communication must occur with an open mindset that we do not know what they are thinking and with the goal to find out exactly what they are thinking. If you want to know how valuable this can be, think back to the time you got exactly what you wanted for your birthday. How did it make you feel? You remember that feeling to this day. Now remember the time you told everyone exactly what you wanted and you got something else instead. You remember that feeling too, don't you?

If you remember that "No One Thinks Like You", you can learn how to think like them. This mindset will strengthen business relationships and provide you with customers who want to do business with you. For those that are wondering, those clouds are blueish grey with a hint of golden sunshine.
