On Difficult Conversations

The Silent Rift: When Leaders Avoid Tough Conversations, Everyone Loses

What actually happens to a team when a leader keeps choosing comfort over the conversation that needs to happen.

By Jared Scott

There's a post circulating on social media that says, "You know someone has done you wrong when they avoid you." It's a statement that hits hard, especially for leaders. Avoidance can create silent divides, misunderstandings, and strained relationships—especially when a team senses something is amiss.

In leadership, this avoidance often comes into play when delivering difficult news. Leaders may delay, hoping the situation will resolve itself, or fear the reaction they might face. But ironically, this delay can lead to exactly what they were trying to avoid: a rift that grows wider with each passing day.

The Cost of Avoidance: A Personal Story

I once had a manager who, for months, seemed to dodge meaningful conversations. At first, I brushed it off. But as time went on, their avoidance felt intentional, almost as if they were concealing something important. The atmosphere became tense, and I found myself questioning everything—had I done something wrong? Was my position at risk? My mind ran wild, conjuring up worst-case scenarios because, in the absence of information, that's often where our thoughts go.

Eventually, the manager was forced to share the news they'd been sitting on. Ironically, the reality was far less severe than I'd imagined. The relief was instant, but the months of tension had already done their damage. What could have been a quick conversation turned into an enduring issue, all because of hesitation.

How Leaders Can "Avoid the Avoidance"

To prevent such misunderstandings, leaders must learn to tackle tough conversations head-on. Here's how:

  1. Embrace Transparency – Share what you can, even if it's uncomfortable. When team members feel informed, they're less likely to assume the worst.
  2. Act Quickly – Delaying tough conversations only allows fear and uncertainty to fester. Address issues promptly, showing that you value honesty over avoidance.
  3. Create a Culture of Openness – Encourage your team to voice concerns, and lead by example. When leaders model transparency, teams are more likely to follow suit.
  4. Communicate Intentions – Sometimes, decisions may be perceived as "wrong" or unfair but explaining your reasoning can help bridge that gap. Teams respect leaders who trust them with the "why" behind tough calls.

A Powerful Quote to Ponder

John C. Maxwell once said, "A leader is one who knows the way, goes the way, and shows the way." If we want our teams to trust us, we must show them that we're willing to handle the hard parts of the job—including difficult conversations.

By choosing openness over avoidance, leaders not only maintain trust but strengthen it—even when delivering unwelcome news. The next time you find yourself hesitating, remember that the short-term discomfort is worth the long-term gain of a resilient, trusting team.
