On Small Things

Beware the $0.25 Tortilla Problem

A tiny cost-cutting decision becomes a case study in how small things quietly damage trust.

By Jared Scott

Leadership is often about the big picture—grand visions, strategic plans, and significant decisions that chart the course of an organization. But sometimes, the smallest decisions can lead to the biggest fallout. Enter Mack Hollins, former Miami Dolphins wide receiver, and his principled stand against a $0.25 tortilla charge.

As told by Ryan Fitzpatrick, then a teammate, Mack Hollins had a daily ritual of eating at Chipotle. One day, Chipotle decided to charge him $0.25 extra for an additional tortilla. Now, Mack Hollins isn't just any customer. He's an NFL player with contracts that most of us can only dream about. Yet, when confronted with that seemingly minor charge, he quit going to Chipotle—permanently. To him, it wasn't about the money; it was about principle. And Chipotle lost a loyal customer, all for a quarter.

This story, while humorous, is a perfect illustration of how small decisions, when not carefully thought through, can have outsized consequences. Leaders, beware the $0.25 tortilla problem.

The Danger of Overlooking the Little Things

In leadership, small changes can have ripple effects that impact your team, your culture, and your organization. Here are some examples of how minor tweaks can lead to significant problems:

  1. Policy Changes That Alienate Imagine your company decides to charge employees $5 for parking at work instead of covering the cost. It's not a large amount, but to employees, it feels like a punishment for showing up. Morale dips, and resentment builds—all for a parking fee.
  2. Perceived Penny-Pinching A company decides to downgrade the office coffee to a cheaper brand. Employees grumble, not because they expect luxury but because the small change signals a lack of appreciation for their comfort.
  3. Communication Mishaps A slight tweak in how leaders communicate—like shortening meeting times to save on costs or using a more transactional tone—can make employees feel undervalued, leading to disengagement.
  4. Ignoring Feedback Small but meaningful employee requests, like better-quality tools or flexible work hours, may seem inconsequential to leadership. Yet, ignoring them can create feelings of being unheard, eroding trust and loyalty.

Why Leaders Need to Think Before They Tweak

The $0.25 tortilla problem teaches us that what seems small to leadership can feel monumental to employees or customers. People don't just respond to the change itself—they respond to what it represents. A small policy shift might inadvertently signal disrespect, stinginess, or a lack of empathy.

Here's the key lesson: What you see as a minor adjustment might be the last straw for someone else.

The Small Things Matter

This principle is well illustrated by a quote often attributed to the poet William Blake: "Great things are done by a series of small things brought together." But the inverse is also true: Great things can be undone by a series of small mistakes.

A Quarter's Worth of Wisdom

Mack Hollins and his $0.25 tortilla problem might make us laugh, but it's a cautionary tale for leaders. The seemingly insignificant decisions we make—policies, charges, or changes—can create lasting impressions. Chipotle lost a customer in Hollins, not because of the quarter itself but because of what that charge symbolized to him.

Leaders, think before you tweak. Ask yourself, "Am I creating a $0.25 tortilla problem?" The answer could save you more than just a quarter—it could save your reputation, your culture, or even your business.
