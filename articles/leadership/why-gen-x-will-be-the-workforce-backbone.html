Why Gen X, Not Millennials, Will Be the Workforce Backbone When Baby Boomers Retire

The overlooked generation that may end up holding the workforce together as Boomers retire.

By Jared Scott

The Baby Boomers, a generation of experienced professionals, are retiring at an alarming rate, leaving behind massive knowledge gaps and a significant void in the workforce. Many assume that Millennials or even Gen Z will naturally step up to fill these roles. But here's a fact that may ruffle some feathers: Generation X—not Millennials—is the generation that leaders must prioritize if they want to maintain productivity and continuity in the workforce.

The Mass Exodus: Boomers Are Leaving

According to Pew Research, roughly 10,000 Baby Boomers turn 65 every day and are exiting the workforce in record numbers. While some organizations may scramble to fill the void with younger workers, the assumption that Millennials and Gen Z can seamlessly take over may be misguided. Younger workers are indeed ambitious and digitally native, but they lack the institutional knowledge, resilience, and loyalty that Gen X has spent decades building.

"It's not that Millennials aren't skilled; it's that Gen X has the experience," says leadership expert Scott Lynch. "Experience cannot be downloaded."

A Funny Reminder of Gen X Resilience

Let's get one thing straight: Gen X has been toughened by everything from latchkey childhoods to adapting to technology halfway through their careers. Picture this: a Gen X-er sitting at a family dinner, casually explaining to their own kids that yes, they used a rotary phone, and if they missed their favorite show, they had to wait for reruns—no instant streaming here! Gen X is that generation that knows how to navigate change without a safety net. They've done it before and, whether they like it or not, they're poised to do it again as Boomers retire.

Why Generation X Holds the Keys to Stability and Growth

Gen X may be a smaller generation, but they are uniquely positioned to provide the stability that organizations desperately need right now. Here's why:

  1. Loyalty and Experience: Unlike Millennials and Gen Z, who are known for frequent job changes, Gen X employees have demonstrated a remarkable level of loyalty. According to a 2022 report from the Bureau of Labor Statistics, Gen X workers have the longest average tenure among all generations.
  2. Prepared for Crisis: Gen X has navigated multiple economic downturns and technological shifts. This resilience has shaped them into adaptable, steady workers who know how to weather storms—a trait companies need as they face uncertain economic and global conditions.
  3. In the Prime of Their Careers: Gen X is currently in their peak working years, equipped with both experience and the energy to lead. They are seasoned but not yet ready to exit the workforce, making them invaluable assets for the long-term.

Competitive Compensation: The Price of Retaining Gen X Talent

As organizations face a shrinking pool of experienced talent with Baby Boomers retiring, the competition to retain and attract Generation X workers is intensifying. Here's a hard truth: retaining Gen X is going to cost more, and it's worth it.

The days of offering minimal annual raises may be over if companies hope to keep Gen X from seeking better offers elsewhere. According to recent surveys, average salary increases across industries are projected at around 4-5% in 2024, a figure that's higher than previous years but may still fall short when it comes to retaining highly skilled, experienced Gen X workers. Compensation consultancy firm Mercer suggests that roles requiring specialized knowledge may see increases closer to 8-10% to retain top talent, especially in sectors facing acute labor shortages.

"When companies treat wages as fixed costs instead of investments, they risk losing the very people who can drive success,"

notes economist Sarah Lockman, an advisor on workforce planning.

Action Steps: How Leaders Can Empower and Retain Gen X Workers

To harness the power of Gen X, leaders need to act decisively. Here are practical steps for ensuring that Gen X remains committed and empowered as the workforce transitions:

  1. Offer Meaningful Benefits: With many Gen X workers sandwiched between caring for both children and aging parents, flexible schedules, mental health support, and retirement planning assistance are powerful retention tools.
  2. Empower Through Upskilling: While Gen X may be less digitally native than their Millennial counterparts, they are highly adaptable. Invest in training that enhances their digital skills and prepares them for the latest innovations.
  3. Recognize and Reward Loyalty: In an era where younger workers may prioritize their personal brands over company loyalty, showing Gen X workers that their loyalty is valued can go a long way. Leaders who reward tenure and recognize dedication will see higher retention and morale.

The Cost of Ignoring Gen X

If organizations fail to retain their Gen X workers, they risk losing not only experienced leaders but also the organizational stability that only comes from continuity. Research shows that the cost of turnover for senior employees can be up to twice their annual salary, considering recruitment, training, and lost productivity. Imagine the cumulative impact if we neglect this generation's value.

Preparing for the Future: Gen X as the Bridge

Looking ahead, it's clear that Gen X will serve as a bridge—carrying forward the institutional knowledge of the Baby Boomers while helping transition organizations to a Millennial and Gen Z workforce. But this won't happen automatically. It's up to today's leaders to make bold choices that prioritize Gen X, ensuring they have the support, tools, and recognition to lead.

Conclusion

The future of the workforce doesn't hinge on Millennials or Gen Z. The real backbone, the generation that will hold things together in this turbulent transition, is Generation X. Bold as it may sound, prioritizing Gen X isn't just smart—it's necessary for organizational stability, productivity, and long-term growth. Leaders must be ready to think differently about generational priorities and recognize that sometimes, the "quiet generation" is the one you can't afford to overlook.
