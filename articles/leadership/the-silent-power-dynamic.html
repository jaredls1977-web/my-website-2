On Favoritism

The Silent Power Dynamic: When Favoritism Hinders Growth

Why quietly favoring your best performers can quietly cost you the growth of everyone else on the team.

By Jared Scott

In many organizations, there exists an invisible yet powerful dynamic: leaders often seem to prioritize their inner circle. These relationships—built on trust, long-standing connections, or a sense of comfort—can lead to decisions that leave other hard-working employees feeling overlooked, despite their dedication.

At the heart of this issue is a critical leadership principle highlighted by Brene Brown:

"Clear is kind. Unclear is unkind."

(Brown, Dare to Lead)

When leaders are unclear about why certain people are given opportunities or why others are passed over, it creates confusion and resentment within the team. A lack of transparency in decision-making not only hinders individual growth but also undermines trust across the organization.

This dynamic doesn't always come from malicious intent. Often, leaders default to supporting those they know or trust, not realizing the impact this favoritism has on the rest of the team. However, when favoritism persists, it stunts innovation and drives away valuable talent who feel their contributions go unnoticed.

Brene Brown's wisdom reminds us that clarity is key to effective leadership. Leaders who are clear about expectations, opportunities, and advancement criteria help build an environment where everyone knows where they stand. This kind of transparency prevents misunderstandings and ensures that hard work, not just personal connections, is rewarded.

So how can organizations ensure they foster this clarity?

  1. Set Clear Expectations: Employees need to understand the criteria for success and advancement. When leaders communicate openly about what is expected, it prevents any sense of ambiguity.
  2. Fair Recognition: Recognizing and rewarding achievements should be based on merit, not relationships. Leaders can build trust by being transparent about why someone is being promoted or recognized.
  3. Empower Everyone: As John C. Maxwell puts it, "Leaders become great not because of their power, but because of their ability to empower others." By empowering the entire team, leaders create an environment where everyone has the opportunity to grow, not just a select few.

Favoritism has been a part of leadership culture for generations, but it doesn't have to dictate the future. By embracing clarity and transparency, leaders can unlock the full potential of their teams. When everyone understands the path to success and feels supported, the organization as a whole becomes stronger and more innovative.

At the end of the day, leaders have a responsibility to be kind through clarity—because "unclear is unkind."

The Power of Clear Communication: Lessons from the Café on Trade

In the heart of downtown Greer, South Carolina, there's a small, unassuming spot called the Café on Trade. For anyone seeking a memorable breakfast experience, this place is truly something special. The café serves up everything you'd want for a perfect Southern breakfast—think fluffy eggs, toast, and the best grits you'll ever taste. Trust me, these grits might even put your grandmother's to shame!

But the food isn't the only thing that makes this café worth visiting. Eddie, the friendly and down-to-earth owner, is often found chatting with customers, preparing dishes, and handling just about everything himself, as is typical in a small, independently run business. Eddie's presence gives the place a welcoming charm, and his straightforward, no-nonsense style extends beyond conversation—it's written on every table.

The Café Rules

Enter the "café rules." These aren't just guidelines; they're Eddie's way of setting clear expectations for behavior, covering everything from basic etiquette to what patrons can expect from their experience. Some might find the rules amusing, but there's a lesson here that leaders can appreciate: clearly defined expectations make for smoother interactions and better relationships.

In too many workplaces, "unwritten rules" dominate the culture. People are often left to figure things out for themselves, which can lead to confusion, frustration, and even conflict. At the Café on Trade, however, there's no room for guesswork. The rules tell you exactly what's expected, leaving no room for misunderstandings.

Leaders Can Learn from Café Rules

This experience offers a simple yet powerful reminder for leaders: clear, concise communication is essential, even if the message may not be what others want to hear. It's tempting to soften our words or avoid certain topics altogether, but that often leads to misinterpretation. In leadership, sometimes people don't need to hear what's comfortable—they need to hear what's necessary.

When we communicate in a way that's both clear and direct, we foster trust. Team members know exactly where they stand and understand what's expected. There's no ambiguity, no guessing games. And while it might not always be easy to deliver tough feedback or enforce guidelines, doing so builds a foundation of mutual respect and integrity.

More Than Just a Breakfast Spot

The Café on Trade serves as more than a breakfast spot; it's a reminder of the value in straightforward communication. As leaders, we may want to create an environment that feels open and friendly, and that often means being honest—even if the truth might ruffle some feathers. Setting clear expectations can prevent a lot of issues before they even start.

So, if you're ever in Greer, SC, stop by this café, enjoy the food, and take in the experience of an environment where everyone knows the rules. And while you're there, do yourself a favor and order the grits—they're worth the trip alone.

Who knew there could be so much to learn from a simple Saturday morning breakfast?

By applying the principles of clear and direct communication, we can build a stronger, more trusting environment for our teams, much like the one Eddie has cultivated at the Café on Trade.
