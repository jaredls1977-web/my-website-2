Let Them Sway: Why Great Leaders Embrace the Winds of Growth

Why the leaders who bend under pressure outlast the ones who insist on standing rigid.

By Jared Scott

Have you ever stood outside on a breezy day and watched the trees sway in the wind? Their branches bend and twist, sometimes to the point where you wonder, "How can they be so flexible without breaking?" What you might not realize is that this very motion—the stress and resistance of the wind—is what makes trees stronger. The wind challenges them, forcing their roots to dig deeper and their trunks to grow sturdier.

But what happens when there is no wind?

This question was answered in the early days of Biosphere 2, a scientific experiment in Arizona designed to replicate Earth's ecosystems in a controlled environment. Inside this enclosed dome, conditions were perfect: abundant sunlight, optimal soil nutrients, and water in all the right amounts. Trees grew quickly in this idealized environment. But as they matured, something unexpected happened—they began to fall over.

The problem wasn't disease or neglect. It was the absence of wind. Without the resistance of the wind, the trees failed to develop "stress wood," the specialized fibers that give them the strength and flexibility to withstand the forces of nature. In this perfectly managed environment, the trees were, ironically, set up to fail.

Now think about how this parallels leadership.

The Danger of Over-Management

Like the controlled environment of Biosphere 2, micromanagement creates conditions that seem perfect on the surface. Leaders who hover over every decision and tightly control their team's actions might see quick results and minimal errors in the short term. Tasks get done their way, deadlines are met, and mistakes are avoided—at first. But in the long run, this approach leads to an environment where employees fail to develop the critical skills they need to handle challenges independently.

When we, as leaders, don't allow our team to make decisions, face challenges, or even make mistakes, we rob them of the opportunity to grow. Just like trees without wind, they don't develop the strength and resilience to stand on their own. And when they falter under pressure, it's not their failure—it's ours as leaders.

The Power of Letting Go

Great leaders understand that struggle is an essential part of growth. They trust their team to sway in the wind, to face challenges, and to develop the "stress wood" of problem-solving, creativity, and resilience. When we give our teams room to grow, we see not just individual improvement but a stronger, more capable organization.

Think about your own team. Are you the wind, challenging and supporting them as they grow stronger? Or are you the biodome, shielding them from every difficulty and inadvertently setting them up to fail?

Victor Lipman put it perfectly in Psychology Today:

"Micromanaging stifles creativity, dampens motivation, and reduces productivity. It can also lead to increased turnover, which is naturally costly and inefficient."

How to Be the Wind

Leadership isn't about controlling every detail—it's about creating an environment where challenges are seen as opportunities, not threats. To be the wind that strengthens your team, consider these steps:

  1. Let Them Sway: Why Great Leaders Embrace the Winds of GrowthTrust Your Team: Delegate decisions and tasks, even if you think you can do them better.
  2. Encourage Growth: Provide opportunities for employees to step out of their comfort zones.
  3. Accept Mistakes: See them as learning opportunities, not failures.
  4. Be Present, Not Overbearing: Be there to guide and mentor, not to micromanage.

Conclusion

The trees of Biosphere 2 were given everything they needed—except the wind. Don't let your team suffer the same fate. As a leader, your job isn't to shield your team from every difficulty. It's to challenge them, trust them, and let them grow stronger through the winds of life and work.

Who knew there could be so much to learn from watching trees sway in the wind?
