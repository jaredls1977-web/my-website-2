On Authenticity

The Cutting Room Floor of Your Leadership

The gap between the leader on stage and the leader in the hallway is smaller than most of us want to admit, and people can always tell.

By Jared Scott

Let’s stop kidding ourselves.

Most leaders are walking around with a highlight-reel version of themselves. The version that sounds right. Looks right. Feels safe to show in meetings, presentations, and posts.

And then there’s the other version.

The one that shows up when you’re tired. When something breaks. When you’re under pressure. When nobody’s watching.

That’s the cutting room floor.

And that’s the version people actually experience.

People know when the image doesn’t match.

We all know the profile-picture game. The angle. The lighting. The filter doing a little extra work.

You meet the person in real life and think, That’s… different.

Not wrong. Just not the same.

Leadership works the same way.

People can tell when what you preach and what you practice don’t line up. They might not call it out. They might not even be able to explain it. But they feel it.

And once that feeling sets in, trust starts leaking out.

You don’t lose credibility because you’re imperfect. You lose it when your words and behavior don’t recognize each other.

You can talk about accountability. People notice when you dodge it.

You can say “people come first.” People notice when they only come first when it’s convenient.

You can hang values on the wall and repeat them in meetings. Your reactions under pressure tell the real story.

That stuff never makes the highlight reel. But it defines your leadership.

Most leaders aren’t fake. They’re exhausted.

They’re trying to live up to what they think leadership is supposed to look like. So they filter themselves. They hide uncertainty. They perform confidence.

And it creates distance.

People don’t need a flawless leader. They need a consistent one.

Authentic leadership isn’t about oversharing. It isn’t lowering standards. It isn’t being unprepared.

It’s alignment.

It’s when the leader on the stage looks like the leader in the hallway. It’s when your worst day doesn’t feel like false advertising.

Authentic leadership works because people can stop guessing who you really are.

That gap—between the highlight reel and the cutting room floor—is exactly why I wrote Authentic Leadership – What Really Works.

Not to help leaders polish their image. But to help them stop managing one.

Everyone has a cutting room floor. That’s human.

What kills leadership is pretending it isn’t there.

Your team isn’t asking for perfection. They’re asking for honesty.
